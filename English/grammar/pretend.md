No. The difference is not imperative vs declarative.

Both pretend to + verb and pretend that + clause can appear in declarative, imperative, or other sentence types.

The real difference is grammatical structure:

1. Pretend to + verb

Used when the subject is pretending to perform an action or be something.

He pretended to sleep.
She pretended to be sick.
They pretended to know the answer.
2. Pretend that + clause

Used when the thing being imagined is expressed as a full sentence (subject + verb).

He pretended that he was asleep.
She pretended that she was sick.
Let's pretend that we are astronauts.
Comparison

He pretended to be sick. ✅

He pretended that he was sick. ✅

She pretended to know the answer. ✅

She pretended that she knew the answer. ✅

The meaning is nearly the same.

Imperative examples

Both patterns can be imperative:

Pretend to be working. ✅
Pretend that you are working. ✅

Here both are imperative sentences because they are commands.

Declarative examples

Both patterns can be declarative:

He pretended to be working. ✅
He pretended that he was working. ✅

Here both are declarative sentences because they are statements.

So:

pretend to + verb = infinitive structure
pretend that + clause = clause structure
The distinction is not imperative vs declarative. ✅
