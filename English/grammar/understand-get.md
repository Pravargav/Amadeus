# Get vs Understand – Complete English Notes

## 1. I have not understood vs I did not understand vs I don't understand

All three sentences are grammatically correct, but they have slightly different meanings.

If someone has just explained something to you and you haven't understood it, the most natural sentence is:

**"I didn't understand."**

### Difference between the three

| Sentence | Meaning / Usage |
|---|---|
| I haven't understood. | You still don't understand what was explained. |
| I didn't understand. | You couldn't understand what the person explained. |
| I don't understand. | You currently don't understand it. |

### Examples in conversation

**1. I didn't understand. (Most natural)**

- Person: Let me explain how this process works.
- You: Sorry, I didn't understand. Could you explain it again?

**2. I don't understand. (When they are explaining right now)**

- Person: First, we need to configure the data source.
- You: I don't understand this step.

**3. I haven't understood. (Less common in everyday conversation)**

- Person: Is everything clear?
- You: Not completely. I haven't understood the last part.

### Recommendation for office conversations

- "Sorry, I didn't quite understand."
- "I'm not sure I follow."
- "Could you go over that again?"

## 2. I don't get you vs I didn't get you

Both sentences are correct and commonly used in everyday English.

### 1. I don't get you.

**Meaning:** I don't understand what you're trying to say.

Example:

- Person: I think we should change the entire approach.
- You: I don't get you. What exactly do you mean?

It can sometimes sound slightly direct.

### 2. I didn't get you.

**Meaning:** I didn't understand what you just said.

Example:

- Person: We need to finish this before deployment.
- You: Sorry, I didn't get you. Could you repeat that?

### 3. I didn't get that.

**Meaning:** I didn't understand that particular explanation or statement.

Example:

- Person: The system uses an incremental data load.
- You: I didn't get that. Could you explain it again?

### Quick comparison

| Expression | Best used when |
|---|---|
| I don't get it. | You currently don't understand something. |
| I don't get you. | You don't understand someone's meaning. |
| I didn't get it. | You didn't understand an explanation. |
| I didn't get you. | You didn't understand what someone said. |
| I haven't got it. | You still haven't understood something (common in British English). |

## 3. Don't get vs Don't understand vs Didn't get vs Didn't understand

The main difference is that **get** is more conversational and informal, while **understand** is more neutral and professional.

Also:
- Don't refers to the present.
- Didn't refers to the past.

### Difference between the four expressions

| Expression | Meaning | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| I don't get it. | I can't understand it. | Informal, present |
| I don't understand. | I can't understand it. | Neutral, present |
| I didn't get it. | I couldn't understand it. | Informal, past |
| I didn't understand. | I couldn't understand it. | Neutral, past |

### Understand through real-life situations

Imagine your colleague is explaining an SAP process.

**Situation A: They are explaining something right now.**

- "I don't get it." – I can't follow your explanation.
- "I don't understand." – I am unable to understand what you're explaining.

Both are correct.

**Situation B: They have already explained something.**

- "I didn't get it." – I couldn't follow your explanation.
- "I didn't understand." – I couldn't understand what you explained.

Both are correct.

### Get vs Understand: Is there a difference?

Yes, slightly.

- **Get:** Understand the meaning, idea or point. More conversational.
- **Understand:** Comprehend the information, explanation or concept. More neutral and versatile.

Examples:

| Situation | Natural expression |
|---|---|
| Someone makes a joke | I don't get it. |
| Someone explains a technical concept | I don't understand. |
| Someone speaks too quickly | I didn't get that. |
| Someone explains a complicated topic | I didn't understand that. |
| Someone gives an unclear answer | I don't get what you mean. |

## 4. What should you use at work?

Here is a useful ranking for professional conversations:

1. "Sorry, I didn't quite understand."
2. "Sorry, I didn't quite get that."
3. "I'm not sure I follow."
4. "Could you explain that again?"

### One additional expression

**"I get it now."**

Meaning: You understand something that you previously couldn't understand.

Example:

- Colleague: The data is loaded through an incremental process.
- You: Oh, I get it now!

---

## 5. Remember this simple rule

| Present | Past |
|---|---|
| I don't get it. | I didn't get it. |
| I don't understand. | I didn't understand. |

- **Don't get / don't understand:** You currently don't understand.
- **Didn't get / didn't understand:** You didn't understand something that was said or explained earlier.

**Final tip:** Use "Sorry, I didn't quite understand" when speaking to colleagues or seniors. It sounds natural, polite and professional.
