# 📊 Budget vs Planning vs Forecast vs Rolling Forecast  
*(When Companies Do Them – Majority Practice)*

---

## 1️⃣ Budget

### What it is
- A **fixed financial plan** for the full upcoming year
- Approved by **leadership**
- **Rarely changed** once approved
- Defines:
  - Revenue targets
  - Cost limits
  - Hiring plans
  - Investments

### When companies do it
➡️ **Yearly (Annual)**  

Most companies do budgeting **once a year**, usually between:
- **September – December** (for the next year)
- Some large enterprises start as early as **July**

### Why yearly
- Sets the **baseline** for the entire fiscal year
- Used for:
  - Board approval
  - Funding allocation
  - Performance targets

---

## 2️⃣ Planning

### What it is
A **broader term** covering all financial planning activities:
- Annual plan
- Operational plan
- Workforce (manpower) plan
- Sales plan
- Capital (CapEx) plan

### When companies do it
➡️ **Mostly yearly**, but parts can be **quarterly or monthly**

### Examples
- Manpower planning → **Reviewed quarterly**
- Operational expense planning → **Annual**, reviewed quarterly

### Majority practice
- Planning is **aligned with budgeting** → **Yearly**
- Adjustments may happen **quarterly**

---

## 3️⃣ Forecast

### What it is
A **best estimate** of how the rest of the year will perform based on:
- Latest actuals
- Market conditions
- Sales pipeline
- Risks & opportunities

### When companies do it
➡️ **Monthly or Quarterly**  
➡️ **Majority trend: Monthly**

Most organizations update forecasts **every month** to stay aligned with market changes.

### Why more frequent
- Actuals change **monthly**
- Helps leadership **avoid surprises**

---

## 4️⃣ Rolling Forecast

### What it is
A **continuous forecast** that always looks ahead a fixed number of periods, such as:
- 12-month rolling forecast
- 18-month rolling forecast
- 24-month rolling forecast

Instead of forecasting only until December, it **adds an extra month every period**.

### When companies do it
➡️ **Monthly** (most common)  
➡️ **Quarterly** (less common)

### Majority trend
- Large companies and **modern finance teams** use **monthly rolling forecasts**
- Provides **real-time forward visibility**
